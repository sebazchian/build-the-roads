# build the roads - The Story

## Who Will Build the Roads?

People ask this whenever someone suggests doing things without a government taking money by force. The trash piles up, the steps break, the walls need paint. Who fixes it if nobody is forced to pay?

The question makes a bad assumption. It thinks people do not want clean streets or working lights. They do. What they lack is a way to show each other they are serious about paying, and a way to find someone who will do the work.

Bounty does that.

Twenty neighbors each pledge 5,000 sats. Nobody can hire a worker alone. Together they have 100,000 sats. A teenager sees the bounty, does the work, uploads a photo, and gets paid. The street gets cleaned. Nobody was forced. Everyone chose.

## The Responsibility Poem

You have probably seen this old rhyme:

> There was an important job to be done and Everybody was sure that Somebody would do it.
> Anybody could have done it, but Nobody did it.
> Somebody got angry about that because it was Everybody's job.
> Everybody thought Anybody could do it, but Nobody realized that Everybody wouldn't do it.
> It ended up that Everybody blamed Somebody when Nobody did what Anybody could have done.

This is what happens when nobody owns the task. Everyone walks past the trash because fixing it is "someone else's job."

Bounty adds three things the poem left out:

1. A price. The job is no longer abstract. "100,000 sats. Clean the lot by Friday." Now the work has a number.
2. A claim. A real person taps "I will do this." Now we know who is responsible.
3. A memory. The app tracks who pledged and who paid, who claimed and who delivered. Nobody hides anymore because the board keeps the score.

The poem ends in blame. Bounty ends in a paid worker and a cleaner street.

## How the Loop Works

Community needs and community labor usually do not talk to each other. The dirty park sits on one side. The unemployed teenager who would gladly work sits on the other. Nothing connects them.

Bounty is the connection. It makes the loop close:

Neighbors pledge sats. Worker claims and delivers. Neighbors pay out. The worker's score goes up. The next time they claim a bounty, people trust them more. The neighbors who paid also score higher. Next time they pledge, workers trust their money is real.

Each loop makes the next one easier.

## Why Bitcoin

You cannot pledge ten cents on a website. The fees eat it. But you can pledge 150 sats. Lightning makes small payments work. The small payment is the whole point. It lets everyone participate at the level they can afford, and the sum of those small pledges builds the road.

This belongs in places like Bitcoin Ekasi, a township in Mossel Bay where Bitcoin is already how people get paid and how shops accept money. The trust is already there, face to face. Bounty just gives it a ledger.

That is what freedom money is for. Not speculation. Getting the road built together, paid in full.
